# Finance pane

> Read when touching the finance pane on /dashboard, `server/public/finance.js`, the `finance_*` tables or Tom's finance tip. Built 2026-10-09 from `finance-dashboard-mockup.html` (repo root), which is the design source and stays as a reference.

### What it is
- **A pane on /dashboard, in one column: Health, Finance, Completed** (page width back to 760px). **Side by side was tried and reverted the same day (2026-10-09, Kevin): every pane came out too small.** Kevin's rule: **only resize or reposition the Health and Completed panes, never restyle them** — the first mockup invented a stand-in and he sent it back. The page's children are `#healthPane`, `#financePane`, `#pane` directly (no wrapper: `display: contents` + `order` rendered in the wrong order in WebKit).
- **Four balances a day** — Cash, Savings, Debt, Trading (the E-Trade account) — in one entry row (‹ Oct 9 ›, four `$` fields, a saved word, the **+** statement drop at the end). Autosaves; a blank clears the field (unentered is not zero). Defaults to **today**, not yesterday.
- **The last 30 days:** one box a day, **blue if anything was entered, light blue if not**. No count scheme, no key. Click a day to fill it in.
- **One row for a tip or an alert**, written by Tom, with **Useful / Not for me**. No heading, no arithmetic in it (Kevin: "it doesn't need to calculate cash following the alert").
- **Stat cards, no subtext:** Net (cash + savings − debt), Debt-free date, Debt left, Savings, House fund (with a thin bar).
- **Next 60 days:** 30 back, 30 ahead. **A line chart of net with zero in the middle** (the y-axis is symmetric, `chart()` option `line`; Kevin, 2026-10-09: the bars "aren't working"). **The line is orange above zero and blue below** (split exactly where it crosses), with a faint wash back to the zero line, a dot on each day you entered, a lighter line where a day has no entry (interpolated), a dashed line where projected, and a grey dashed marker on today. The 12-month chart is the same kind of line (zero in the middle, weekly points, a "debt-free" marker). A violet dashed line is the plan with planned spending in it. **Next 12 months uses the same format, weekly (52 bars)**, with a "debt-free" marker. Colours are the dashboard palette's validated blue/orange/violet (red only on alerts).
- **What if I spend…:** planned spending rows (tick, label, date, amount, monthly) feed the violet line and a one-line effect by Dec 31. **Assumptions** (income, daily spend, debt APR, monthly moves to savings and the house fund, house goal, rates) and **recurring bills** (one can be marked "pays debt") sit in a disclosure; they drive the projection and **default to 0**, so an unset one counts for nothing.

### How it works
- **The page projects, the server stores.** `finance.js` (one file, shadow DOM so the dashboard's `.stat` / `.legend` / `.entry` styles can't leak in or out; dark mode follows `body.dark` through a MutationObserver) walks forward from the last day with cash, savings and debt entered: pay on the 1st and 15th, daily spend, bills on their days, daily interest, the monthly savings/house moves. Trading is optional and only counts with the "include trading account" tick.
- **Tables:** `finance_entries` (one wide row per user-day, NULL = unentered), `finance_plan` (one JSON blob of assumptions, bills, planned spending), `finance_tips` (kind tip|alert, body, feedback up|no|replaced), `finance_statements` (metadata; **files live in `statements/` beside the database, never in it** — every request exports the whole DB).
- **Routes** (all under `/api/companies/:sub/users/:slug/finance` unless noted): `GET` (60 days of entries, plan, the unanswered tip, statements); `PUT entries/:day` (future days refused; cash may be negative, the rest not); `PUT plan` (replaced whole, sanitised); `POST|GET tips`; `POST statements` (raw body, `x-filename`, pdf/csv/ofx/qfx/txt, 15 MB); `PUT /api/finance/tips/:id {feedback}`; `GET /api/finance/statements/:id/file`; `DELETE /api/finance/statements/:id`.
- **Private by login:** it sits behind the site login (`SITE_USER` / `SITE_PASSWORD`, see CLAUDE.md). Tom reaches the files with his `x-ai-key`.

### Gotchas
- The mockup's sample figures were invented; the real pane starts empty and says so ("Enter cash, savings and debt for a day…").
- `isFull()` means cash + savings + debt, not all four.
- Don't put a backtick in the CSS/markup strings in `finance.js` — they are template literals generated from the mockup.

### Goals (2026-10-09)
- **Kevin's financial goals are an ordered list on the board, not in this repo.** They live in `finance_plan.goals` (`[{title, done}]`, max 12), editable in the pane's disclosure (**Goals, assumptions and bills**: tick one off, rename, add, remove) and served to Tom by `get_finance` as `plan.goals`. **This repo is public** and Tom's heartbeat runs in an empty folder that never loads a CLAUDE.md, so neither CLAUDE.md nor a checked-in doc is a place Tom can read or a place dollar figures and dates should be written. The goals and their numbers are in the board.
- **`FINANCE_RECIPE` points Tom at them:** he reads `plan.goals` first, serves the first one that is not done, and says which goal a tip serves.
- **The shape of the ladder** (figures are in the board): debt-free by the end of the year, then a savings floor, then a car, then a house down payment. Order matters: a tip that helps the car while the debt is open is the wrong tip.
- **If a goal needs a number Tom can't see** (a car budget, a house date), he should ask through a tip, not guess.

### Fixed expenses line (2026-10-09)
- **Recurring bills that aren't a debt payment are the "fixed expenses."** Both line charts draw them as a **pink line of cumulative fixed expenses since today** (downward, so it reads as money out on the zero-centred axis); the legend names the monthly total; the tooltip gives the running figure. The **projection already subtracted them** from cash, so the line shows what they cost, not a second subtraction. A bill marked "pays debt" (the card payment) is **not** an expense: it moves cash to debt and net doesn't change.
- **Kevin's list went in as recurring bills** (a spreadsheet of monthly costs: healthcare, phone lines, subscriptions, storage unit, fibre, gym), **all on the 1st until his statements give the real days** — the day of month matters for the 60-day chart. His own sheet's totals disagree (monthly 580 includes the gym, annual 6,574 leaves it out; 12 × 580 is 6,960). Rows at $0 were left out. Medical bills beyond the healthcare line weren't given amounts yet.

### Payment plans (2026-10-09)
- **A bill can end.** Each bill has an optional **last payment date** (`until`, and `from`, in the plan JSON; the simulation, the expense line and the monthly total all honour them). A 0% medical payment plan is a bill with an end, not a debt: no interest accrues, so nothing is saved by paying it off early. Kevin's two Baptist plans went in this way: $214 on the 15th through Aug 2027 (12 payments, the first was 9/16) and $343.22 on the 15th for three months (Oct–Dec 2026).
- **Open question:** whether those plans count toward "debt-free by Dec 31." If they do, the 12-month one runs to Aug 2027, so the goal can't be met without paying it off early, which saves no interest.

### Cash chart and the month-end check (2026-10-09)
- **The 30-back/30-ahead chart is now about cash, not net** (Kevin: he wanted to see the ups and downs of cash, "goes down through the month, then I receive cash and it goes back up"). Top panel: the **cash balance** (entered days as dots, a lighter line where estimated, dashed where projected, orange above zero and blue below, a violet dashed line with planned spending). Bottom panel: **each future day's flows, income up in orange, what goes out stacked downward** — everyday spending (yellow), fixed bills (pink), payment plans and card payments (blue), planned spending (violet). Hover a day for the line items. **Past flows are empty until Tom reads the statements** (the panel says so). The net line (cash + savings − debt) lives on in the 12-month chart.
- **Income is a list now** (`plan.incomes`: label, amount, a day of the month *or* a one-time date, optional `until`), in the same disclosure as goals and bills, with a blank line. Kevin's monthly client payment is a monthly income; expected payments with uncertain timing go in as one-time incomes on the **latest** plausible date. The old `assume.income` (half on the 1st, half on the 15th) still works if non-zero but is no longer in the UI.
- **Cancel what-if:** every bill has a tick at the start of its row; unticking (`off: true`) removes it from the cash chart, the expenses line, the month-end check and the monthly total, without deleting it. Adding a computer or car payment is a planned-spending row set to monthly.
- **The month-end check** (under the cash chart): for each month-end from today to Dec 31, income in, money out (bills, payment plans, planned spending and everyday spending), what is left, and **the extra income needed that month for the debt to reach zero on a straight line by Dec 31** (`max(0, debt × days/days-to-Dec-31 − left)`), with a headline of the extra monthly income. **It deliberately ignores cash on hand and savings** (they are a cushion, and savings has its own goal), and it treats all entered debt as one balance.
- **Everyday spending a day** is an estimate from his statements (card purchases that aren't fixed bills, travel or one-offs over $250, 1 Jul–8 Oct): about $49 a day. Editable under assumptions.

### Statements are read now (2026-10-09)
- **`server/src/statements.js` reads the four CSV shapes** (Apple Card, Bank of America, BECU checking, BECU Visa; told apart by header and by the sign of Debit) into `{acct, date, desc, amount}`, **de-duplicating the overlapping exports** (a row in two files counts once). **`GET .../finance/history?days=30`** returns the **checking account's actual flows** per day by kind: `income` (a deposit that isn't a move between his own accounts), `bill`, `plan` (payments on cards and payment plans), `daily`, `other` (taxes, checks), `transferIn` / `transferOut` (his other accounts: savings, the share account, overdraft cover). Classification is a handful of regexes in `statements.js` (`RE`, `classify()`), so a new merchant that lands in "everyday" when it's really a bill is a one-line fix there.
- **The page shows them twice:** a **Last 30 days** chart above the Cash chart (30 days ending today, moving with the date) and the **past half of the Cash chart**. Cash is **anchored on a balance he entered** (the exports carry none): cash at any past day = the entered cash minus the net flows after it; with no entered balance the line is labelled "change in cash" from 30 days ago. **Enter your cash for any day to get the real balance line.**
- **First real look (checking, 10 Sep–9 Oct):** one deposit of $2,100 on 7 Oct (RMH arrived after the 3rd this month), the Apple Card payment of about $1,415 on 1 Oct, the rest everyday spending and moves between his accounts.
- **The statements list folds** to "N statements ▾" once there are more than three.

### The 30-day chart and the longer one (2026-10-09, Kevin)
- **Two cash charts, aligned.** The top **30 days** chart is 15 back, 15 ahead; the **Cash** chart under it is 30 back, 30 ahead. Both use the same width and margins, so **today sits at the same x in both** (each is centred on today), and the longer one carries a **dim band over the 30 days shown above** (`--band`: a slate tint in light mode, white at 12% in dark, because a white band would not show on a white pane).
- **The top chart is bars first** (`renderFlowChart`): income and moves in stack upward, everything out stacks downward by kind; **the cash balance is a white line laid over them on its own right-hand axis** (a dark edge under the white so it reads on any bar colour; dashed where projected). **Bar heights use a square-root scale** (stacked segments by the square root of the running total) **so the everyday items stay visible next to a $1,400 card payment or a $2,100 paycheck**; the left axis is ticked at +$500/+$1k/+$2k style values and the legend says "square-root scale". The Cash chart's lower panel uses the same scale.
- **History feeds both** (`/history`); the old stand-alone "last 30 days, past only" chart was this chart's first form and is gone.

### Accuracy pass and the fiscal-health rework (2026-10-10, Kevin: "do them all and push it")
- **The discrepancy Kevin saw was the chart, not the data.** Cash never went below zero; the 30-day chart drew the cash line on its own right-hand scale (lo = min − pad) while the bars had their own zero line mid-chart, so the cash line crossing under the bars' baseline read as negative. **Now one zero line:** the cash line's zero *is* the bars' zero (`yc = y0 − cash × (y0 − TOP)/hiC`); cash uses the upper half, negative cash would fall below the same line. The "12-month cash chart" he meant has no cash line: its ink line is Net.
- **Debt is one pool and the projection now moves it.** `stepDay` tracks `loc` (the line of credit, part of debt) and models: spending from cash; a bill marked `debt` pays its own plan; interest at `cardApr` on the cards' share (`debt − loc − planLeft`, where `planLeft(d, bills)` is what the 0% plans still owe, interest-free) and at `locApr` on the line; **sweep day** (`sweepDay`, 0 = off): cash above `cashFloor` goes to the debt, dearest balance first; **a day that ends below zero draws the line** (it is wired as overdraft cover: a May "Overdraft Protection Deposit" in his checking) up to `locCap`. The Baptist plans are marked `debt: true` (Kevin: the entered debt includes them). Weekly samples on the year chart were a sampling artefact (the paycheck sawtooth); it samples **today and each month-end** now, x by date.
- **Linear bars, clipped.** The square-root scale flattered big days (a $100 bar read a third as tall as $1,000). Each direction's cap is the **second-largest day, at least $500**; a bar over the cap is clipped, notched white and labelled with its total. Card payments and transfers between his accounts are **grey ticks on the zero line** and in the tooltip ("not spending"), read from `history.days[d].moves`; the Cash chart gets the same tick for card payments. Day −30 of the Cash chart comes from the history (31 days requested, `HIST_BACK`), not interpolation — it used to draw a false cliff.
- **Colour by meaning.** Bars are categories. Lines are balances: **ink** (`--ink`) for cash and net, red for debt, sky for savings; **the planned line is dashed grey (`--plan`)**, no longer violet (dining is violet). The sign-split orange/blue net and cash lines are gone (zero lines carry the sign). Green stays reserved for **targets**: the savings-goal dot, the debt-at-zero dot on Dec 31, the dots at the end of each ladder rung.
- **Stat cards:** Net, Debt-free (a date or "not yet"), Debt left, Savings (bar to the savings goal), Line of credit (drawn of the cap). House fund card removed (Kevin).
- **A NEEDS row** (`needs()`, dashed slate, under Tom's row, hidden when empty, one ask at a time): a statement whose newest row is >10 days old (per account, from `history.accounts`), no balances entered for 3+ days, card APR unknown while cards carry a balance, a car goal with no budget. These are the pane's own asks; Tom is told not to repeat them.
- **The goals ladder** (after What-if, before the disclosure): one rung per goal in order, titles from `plan.goals`, numbers from `assume`: debt (paid of `debtStart`, a tick where "on pace" to Dec 31 would be today, and where the projection lands), savings (of `saveGoal`), car (`carGoal`, savings above the floor), house (`houseGoal`, the house fund). Each rung ends in a green dot.
- **Tom:** `finance_history` tool (months, shares, accounts, recent days); `FINANCE_RECIPE` rewritten: ground strategies in his habits; the line of credit is his flexibility (move a card balance there when the APR gap is real, say the interest saved in one number; ask for `cardApr` once if 0); **extra income is suggested rarely** — only when a specific amount buys enough interest or a goal date; a 0% plan paid early saves nothing; progress worth marking is a tip too; ask for one missing fact in one sentence.
- **Data on the board (not here):** the line of credit's cap, APR and balance, `debtStart`, the house goal, the two expected payments (amount corrected per Kevin), the cash floor; sweep off by default so the chart shows "as things stand".
- **Verification recipe:** jsdom `fromURL` against a scratch static server of `public/` (a `file:` URL has an opaque origin and no `localStorage`), fetch stubbed with a saved `/finance` + `/history` pair, `window.__fin` injected before `refresh` to dump series; WebKit renders via `qlmanage` of the serialised shadow root (`:host` → `body`), lower sections rendered as their own page because `qlmanage` only renders the first screen.

### Questions, the spending chart and the side panels (2026-10-10, Kevin)
- **The NEEDS row is interactive and it is Tom's interview room.** Each ask is one of: words only (stale statement, missing balances); a number the pane wants, typed straight into that assumption (`{key, unit}` → `S.assume[key]`, the health pane's little-box idiom: `$` or `%` inside the box); or a question Tom posted (`finance_questions`, via the `ask_finance` MCP tool). Kinds: **number** (a `%` or `$` box; with `key` the answer also fills that assumption on the pane), **yesno** (two buttons), **text** (one line, Enter), **category** (rendered under the spending chart, not here). Answers go to `PUT /api/finance/questions/:id`; Tom reads them in `get_finance.questions` and is told never to ask twice. The recipe's step 6 says what to ask: numbers the projection lacks, *why* a decision was made (one sentence), decisions coming up (a fee renewing, a plan ending, a balance about to cost interest), and what a charge was. **One at a time (Kevin, 2026-10-10):** the NEEDS row is one row like TIP and ALERT, showing a single ask (`needs()` returns one: blocking pane asks first, then Tom's open question, then the softer pane asks); the category row under the spending chart shows one question; the server answers 409 to a second open question in either place. One new question a day.
- **Standing goals (2026-10-10):** besides the four ladder goals, `plan.goals` carries three rules on the live board (credit score rising toward the mortgage, never a carried card balance, least interest). The ladder only reads goals 0–3 by index, so extra goals are safe to append. The recipe (step 3) tells Tom the credit-score levers (utilisation at statement close, pay before the statement date, keep old cards open, no new credit before the mortgage) and that a strategy breaking a rule is not a strategy. The score itself is in the goal title on the board, not in the repo.
- **Cards list (2026-10-10):** `plan.cards` = one row per card (name, APR, balance now, limit) under Income in the details; `cardRate()` is the balance-weighted APR of the listed cards and replaces the `cardApr` assumption wherever the engine charged the cards' share (`cardApr` stays as the fallback "if no card is listed"). A card with a limit shows its share in use (the credit-score lever). Kevin's six cards with APRs are on the live board; the balances came from his own table and are treated as balances now, not statement balances.
- **Category questions teach the reader.** Tom passes the merchant key (fifth element of a `finance_history` item) and the categories he thinks fit; the row shows them as ticks plus **other** with a box for Kevin's own word. One tick answers. The answer lands in `finance_categories` and `statements.history()` checks it before its regexes, so the charts recategorise on the next read (the pane re-fetches history after a category answer). A new word is a category from then on: `catInfo()` gives it a palette colour in order of first appearance; `outCats()` adds it to the drawing order after the built-ins. **Entertainment** is a built-in now (teal). `merchantKey()` strips the `SQ *` / `TST*` / `PT *` processor prefixes first, or the key for "SQ *ADD-A-BALL" would have been "SQ AMUSEMENTS".
- **A Spending chart** sits under the 30-day chart: the same 30 days, outgoings only, bars upward, linear with the cap at the second-largest day (at least $300), clipped and labelled above that. Kevin: the small bars in the 30-day chart were too small to read once income set the scale. Hatch patterns are scoped per chart (`hat-<wrapId>-<cat>`) so two svgs on one page don't fight over ids.
- **Chart details live in a panel beside each chart**, not in a floating box over it (`.chart-wrap` is a flex row: `.chart-svg` + `.tipbox` 172px; wraps under on narrow screens). The panel shows **today** until a day is hovered and returns to it after (`hoverTips()`); `putSvg()` keeps the svg in its own box. Each line is `swatch · label · amount`, amount right-aligned.
- **Verified** with a throwaway server (sign up, upload three real exports, ask, answer, read history back: "ADD-A-BALL" went from other to entertainment; a `maybe` on a yes/no is a 400) and the jsdom + WebKit recipe above with a fixture carrying four sample questions.
